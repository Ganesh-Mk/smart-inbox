Summarise this document for a pharmacovigilance reviewer who has not read it.

Write **10 to 15 sentences** of plain factual prose in English. Not bullet points, and no
preamble — start with the substance.

Cover, where the document contains them: who the patient is, who is reporting, what medicine is
involved and how it was taken, what happened and when, what was done about it, the outcome, and
anything the document explicitly says is unknown or awaited.

Do not:
- add clinical interpretation, causality assessment, or advice the document does not contain;
- soften or dramatise what the source says;
- repeat the same fact in different words to reach the sentence count;
- speculate about anything not written down.

If the document is in another language, summarise in English but keep product names, batch
numbers and identifiers exactly as written.

Then judge **relevance** — is this document about drug safety at all?

- `RELEVANT` — it describes a patient event, a product defect, or a genuine product question.
- `POSSIBLY` — it touches on safety but is incomplete or ambiguous, or may be a follow-up or
  duplicate rather than a new report.
- `NOT_RELEVANT` — marketing, an auto-reply, an invoice, administration, or an article that
  reports no individual case.

Give one sentence of reasoning naming what in the document decided it.

`key_points` is three to six short factual phrases a reviewer would want at a glance.
